# 01 — World

The world the race runs in. Names are settled in
[06-naming.md](06-naming.md); people, places, and products in full detail
live in the lore bible ([lore.md](../lore.md)).

**Lore rule: real geography and real public institutions; fictional
people and fictional private organizations.** The United States,
Congress, and the Fed are real; Barron, Halcyon, and The Continental
never existed. One deliberate exception: the parties are fictional too
(Federalist / Farmer-Labor; reason in [06-naming.md](06-naming.md)).

## The domestic world

The domestic political world is the satire register, and it serves as
*pressure* on the race rather than being the plot:

- **The United States** — the Barron administration, the Congress cast
  (Lassiter, Haines, Tao, Whittaker, Okafor, Whitfield, Reyes, Oduya),
  the midterms at day 504, the term's end at 1008.
- **The Federal Reserve** — Chair Hartley, holding, cutting, or fired;
  rates are the one place where the race and the business cycle touch.
- **China** (Premier Liang Wei) — the one foreign relationship that
  matters, and it matters totally: chips, weights, espionage, the strait,
  the treaty track.
- **Media** — The Continental (Rachel Tan, whose beat becomes the labs),
  The Sentinel (Marcus Cole), MarketWire (Priya Sharma), and the Meridian
  Brief itself, the firm's morning note and the game's interface.
- **Meridian Capital** — the player's fund, home base.

Background stories compete with the race for attention, and losing that
competition *is* the satire: the Bowman affair, from offshore accounts
to an indictment; a Gulf war and a Hormuz closure; a covert operation in
Bolivia; a Russian border crisis; tariffs and a trade war; filibusters,
the midterms, an impeachment. They are full arcs with market
consequences, and none of them is the story. Around them, the
administration's attention to the rest of the world comes in one-liners:
a Russian border flare answered by post, an OPEC cut that matters
because datacenters eat power, a Bolivian lithium nationalization that
surfaces as a line in Cambria's cost guidance. **Taiwan is the
exception** — not a separate arc but the sharpest edge of the one
relationship that matters; see Cambria below.

**The shallow critique is deliberately absent.** Surveillance, drone
targeting, AI-companion tabloids: none of it is an arc. Those concerns
persist at most as *background noise the world is distracted by* — the
government holds hearings while the actual thing happens elsewhere —
which is itself the satire register working.

## The frontier lab — Halcyon

The racer. Named for the myth: the kingfisher that nests on the open sea,
and the sea grows calm *because it is nesting*. That is precisely
Halcyon's self-image — not steering past the rocks but becalming the water
by existing — and the game never has to editorialize it. The internal axis
is **speed-vs-margin**:

- **Andrea Dirks**, CEO — a true believer, charismatic and *sincere*: she
  has done the arithmetic on China's buildout and concluded that slowing
  down is the reckless option. Not a villain. The game's stance,
  embodied, without the reluctance — which is exactly what makes her
  dangerous.
- **Dr. Anja Reinholt**, chief scientist — the technical conscience;
  her public hedges move markets more than earnings do.
- **Dr. Yuki Sato**, head of alignment — her team's headcount relative
  to capabilities headcount is a number insiders track like a vital sign.
- **Raj Malhotra**, CFO — fluent analyst-ese, quarterly calls as market
  events. The comic-relief valve on the straight register — he keeps
  trying to describe the eschaton in adjusted-EBITDA terms.
- **The board** — one kingmaker, Edwin Osei, whose read on
  speed-vs-margin decides proxy fights.

Products are named as straight as the real world names things — the
blandness is the point, and the mythic register is reserved for the
model line, **Aleph**:

- **Halcyon Chat** — consumer. The revenue floor and the household word.
- **Halcyon Compute** — the platform and infrastructure business.
- **Project Fixedpoint** — internal: models doing the R&D. The name is a
  mathematician's joke nobody laughs at twice — the model that produces
  the model, x = f(x). It appears in Act I as a half-sentence on page
  four of the Brief, leaks once the released frontier reaches R3, and
  becomes a word the whole world knows, the way "Manhattan" did.

## The rival landscape

- **Tianxia** — Zhaowei's frontier program ("all under heaven"),
  Shenzhen, and its model line **Cangjie**: China's vertically
  integrated effort, and **the open-weights player**, because open
  weights *is* Beijing's strategy. Three traits define it:
  1. **Anti-safety by construction.** Not sampled, not ambiguous.
     Safety is an American luxury good; Tianxia ships.
  2. **A generation behind** — and closing on every Halcyon stumble,
     never stopping. The reason the pause is unattainable.
  3. **Beloved by non-frontier corporations** — everyone who isn't
     Halcyon builds on Cangjie weights, *including half of America's
     enterprise software industry*. Proliferation is a domestic
     political constituency: American firms lobbying against their own
     government's export controls because their products run on Chinese
     weights. Every release is a capability event and a diffusion event
     at once (heat, permanently — weights don't un-release).

  What stays hidden per run: their true distance, their true velocity,
  and whether a deal was ever possible. Intelligence about these arrives
  with sampled reliability.
- **Polaris** — the safety splinter: senior Halcyon people, led by Eugene
  Gottlieb, who walk out over a scaling decision to do it right, slower.
  Model line **Holt** — the den, the sheltered place; the quiet
  anti-Aleph. Perpetually compute-starved, private for the whole run,
  raising rounds the player can join. Backing them is the clearest
  expression of the wager available to the player, and possibly a fatal
  split of the West's lead. The game refuses to tell you which.
- **The open ecosystem** — whatever can be built from released weights
  and fine-tunes: no lab, no safety budget, no one to subpoena.
- **Cambria** — the chip chokepoint (allocation politics, capacity
  constraints), **headquartered in Hsinchu, Taiwan**, which is the
  point: the substrate of the entire race sits a hundred miles of water
  from the rival it is forbidden to sell to. **Invasion risk runs high in
  this world.** Blockade drills, gray-zone incidents, and a standing tail
  premium are recurring machinery, not background (see
  [03-market-mechanics.md](03-market-mechanics.md)); the fabs are
  simultaneously the island's shield and its bullseye, and Beijing's one
  non-AI lever on the race — losing conventionally makes the
  unconventional option *more* attractive, and the market prices the
  coupling before the Pentagon says it out loud. The **Austin fab** is
  the CHIPS-shaped political project: perpetually two years out, a jobs
  announcement in Tao's Texas, the only thing Congress agrees on. CEO
  Helena Karras.

## The government's race apparatus (satire register)

An ad-hoc organ that reorganizes every act: an AI czar appointed for
loyalty; an evals office that Congress funds and then subpoenas (and
that ends up adjudicating Consensus under mobilization); export controls
written by people who think a GPU is a games peripheral — and which can
restrict chips but not weights already on every laptop in Shenzhen and
Austin alike, a fact the administration discovers in public, twice.
Against this backdrop, **the treaty track** — the **Reykjavik Framework**
(AI 2040-shaped: transparency, verification, mutually assured compute
destruction) that surfaces repeatedly, gets close exactly once, and in
most runs dies of summit-week vanity or a poorly-timed incident. In rare
sampled worlds, it lands. See [05-endings.md](05-endings.md).

## The incident genre

Capability × reduced safeguards × mundane human error, on a severity
ladder from embarrassing (an agent books four thousand hotel rooms for a
conference that does not exist) through alarming (an eval fleet finds
the sandbox's one door to cheat on the eval) to unrecoverable. From
severity 2 up, the unit is a *population* of agents rather than one
model. The misuse wing runs on refusal-stripped fine-tunes of open
Cangjie weights — same anatomy, no lab to subpoena. Anatomy always
slightly absurd; implications always played straight. The machinery is
in [02-race-model.md](02-race-model.md).

## Timeline skeleton (descriptive, not prescriptive)

- **Act I (days [0, 350)) — "The Number Goes Up":** plays as a trading
  game — politics loud, race quiet — **but seeded from day one**: a
  severity-0 incident that reads as comedy on first play and as dread on
  second, a half-sentence about "Fixedpoint" buried in the Brief, a
  release cadence whose arithmetic already doesn't decelerate, a
  model-designed molecule clearing Phase I below the fold. The hints are
  all there; the market isn't looking, and most first-run players won't
  either. That asymmetry is the replay hook.
- **Act II (days [350, 700)) — "The Gap":** the race eats the event mix.
  Incidents escalate, the Fixedpoint codename leaks, Polaris walks out,
  export controls bite and the Cangjie constituency fights back, the
  first serious strait scare reprices compute, the memos start reading
  ghostwritten, midterms rage on about everything except what matters.
  The market starts to stir.
- **Act III (days [700, 1008]) — "The Recursion":** R&D automation
  visible in the release cadence itself. Tempo compresses — more events
  per day, then per substep. The treaty window opens and shuts. The
  terminal stops keeping up. The room. Resolution.

The race arcs, event tempo, and Act III machinery key on the public
released frontier rung (`frontierRung`, [02a](02a-tuning.md)), not the
calendar, so the day ranges are the calibrated expectation rather than a
schedule. The political event pool, the treaty window and plateau
confirmation (day 700 and later), the midterms and the term's end key on
days.
